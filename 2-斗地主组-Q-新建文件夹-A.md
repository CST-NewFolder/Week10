# Introduction to Computer Science — Questions on AI



1. *What remains uniquely human?** If AI handles writing, reasoning, design, and decisions faster and cheaper, which human capacities still justify their value — and who decides?

2. **Who is accountable?** When an AI system harms someone — a wrong diagnosis, a denied loan, a biased hire — who is responsible: the builder, the deployer, the user, or no one?

3. **Can we still share a reality?** As models train on human content and then generate much of what humans read and watch, how do we preserve trustworthy knowledge and a common basis for public debate?

4. **What becomes of work and dignity?** If productivity surges while labor demand shrinks, how do people earn a living, and how does a society distribute the gains?

5. **What happens to meaning and motivation?** If effort is no longer required for achievement, what happens to learning, craft, and identity — and how should education be redesigned?

6. **Can we audit what we no longer understand?** How do we keep human agency and oversight when systems are opaque and their behavior emerges from data rather than design?

7. **Whose values get encoded?** Every model embodies choices about what is acceptable. Who sets them, across cultures and jurisdictions, and how can others contest them?

8. **Does capability concentration equal power concentration?** As a handful of firms control compute, data, and models, what happens to competition, democracy, and national sovereignty?

9. **How do we govern something that improves faster than laws?** Should regulation target the technology, the use cases, or the harms — and can any of them keep pace?

10. **What is truth when fluency is free?** When synthetic text, images, audio, and video are indistinguishable from real ones, how do we verify evidence and trust testimony?

11. **Can AI be aligned with something as contested as human flourishing?** Whose definition of "good" does alignment optimize for, and what happens when humans disagree with each other?

12. **Should we slow down, and can we?** Is a pause or a red line technically enforceable and politically possible — or does competition make restraint collectively irrational?
